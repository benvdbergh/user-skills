# Governance And Decision Models

## Focus

This reference supports governance transparency and role clarity in open source projects.

## Governance Minimum Viable Documentation

Document at least:
- roles and responsibilities
- entry and promotion criteria for each role
- decision process (proposal, discussion, decision, appeal)
- conflict resolution and code of conduct escalation path
- succession and continuity plan

## Role Model Baseline

- Contributor: submits issues, reviews, docs, code, support, or operations improvements.
- Maintainer: accountable for project direction, quality bar, and contributor enablement.
- Committer or reviewer: trusted merge or approval authority for defined areas.
- Governance body (optional): steering group or council for high-impact decisions.

Each role should define:
- scope of authority
- expected time commitment
- accountability and behavior expectations
- path to enter, remain, and step down

## Decision-Making Models

Common models:

1. Founder-led (BDFL-style)
   - Fast early decisions.
   - Needs explicit transition path as project grows.

2. Merit-based leadership
   - Influence linked to sustained, high-quality contribution.
   - Requires transparent criteria to avoid perceived cliques.

3. Consensus-seeking with tiebreaker
   - Default for major decisions in diverse communities.
   - Requires clear timeout and final decision authority.

4. Elected governance roles
   - Useful for larger or foundation-backed communities.
   - Needs clear electorate and cadence rules.

## Recommended Decision Protocol

- Propose: issue or discussion with context, options, and trade-offs.
- Discuss: bounded time window with explicit call for concerns.
- Decide: state outcome, rationale, and decider.
- Record: link final decision in `GOVERNANCE.md` or ADR-like log.
- Review: scheduled checkpoint for reversal or adaptation.

## Transparency Safeguards

- Keep governance docs aligned with real behavior.
- Publish leadership roster and role status.
- Include appeal path for process disputes.
- Avoid hidden vetoes and private-only decision channels.
