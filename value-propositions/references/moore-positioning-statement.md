# Moore-style positioning statement (category + contrast)

**When to load:** Crafting a concise **external** statement: who it’s for, what category, primary benefit, and contrast vs alternative—common in B2B tech positioning.

**Primary source (external):** Geoffrey A. Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (whole product and mainstream adoption dynamics). Summaries and training materials often cite a **six-part** formula; wording varies slightly by excerpt.

## Widely cited template shape

A common Moore-style structure is:

1. For **(target customer)**
2. Who **(statement of need/opportunity)**
3. The **(product name)** is a **(product category)**
4. That **(key benefit / compelling reason to buy)**
5. Unlike **(primary competitive alternative)**
6. Our product **(primary differentiation)**

## Agent practices

- Make the **category** truthful (avoid “we are a new category” unless supported).
- “Unlike” must name a **credible alternative** (often status quo, not only a logo competitor).
- Connect differentiation to **observable outcomes** (see `organizational-inputs-and-assumptions.md`).

## Whole product caveat

Moore’s **whole product** idea: mainstream buyers need the complete solution (integrations, services, ecosystem). If the positioning implies completeness, surface dependencies per `skill-escalation.md`.
