# HBR-style discipline — business markets, proof, and “puffery”

**When to load:** Board-level scrutiny, enterprise procurement, or when claims must be **credible** and **documented**—not slogans.

**Primary sources (external):** Classic HBR articles on customer value propositions in business markets (e.g., Anderson & Narus; follow-on HBR pieces on what makes propositions persuasive). A frequently cited entry point:

- [Customer Value Propositions in Business Markets (HBR)](https://hbr.org/2006/03/customer-value-propositions-in-business-markets)

## Core ideas (practical synthesis)

### 1. Resonating vs favorable vs unsubstantiated

A common taxonomy in business-markets writing contrasts:

- **All-benefits** — lists what the supplier believes matters (weak if generic)
- **Favorable points of difference** — highlights differences vs alternatives (better; still needs proof)
- **Resonating focus** — emphasizes one or two **highest-impact** differences tied to the customer’s economics (strong when evidenced)

Use this lens when critiquing existing marketing copy.

### 2. Proof and documentation

HBR-style discipline stresses that business buyers treat unsupported superiority claims skeptically. Translate marketing language into:

- **Demonstrable differences**
- **Customer-specific relevance**
- **Evidence** (even if initially qualitative, be explicit)

### 3. Clarify the comparison baseline

Value claims implicitly compare to something—make it explicit (aligns with NBA thinking in `economic-value-and-pricing-logic.md`).

## Agent practices

- Replace superlatives with **mechanisms** (“reduces integration interfaces” vs “best-in-class”).
- For each major claim, ask: **What would a skeptical buyer ask for?** (pilot metrics, reference architecture, third-party validation)
- Flag “marketing absolutes” (always, never, guaranteed) unless legally approved.

## No-gos

- Passing off opinion as validated customer evidence
- Quoting HBR statistics from memory—ask the user for their source doc if precision matters
