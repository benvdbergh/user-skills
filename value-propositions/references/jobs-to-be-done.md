# Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) — value proposition lens

**When to load:** The user speaks in terms of **customer progress**, **situations**, **struggling moments**, or when feature lists fail to explain **why** buyers switch.

JTBD is a family of methods; common theme: customers “hire” solutions to make progress in a circumstance—often expressed in **functional, emotional, and social** terms.

**When not sufficient alone:** Highly regulated B2B procurement where economic proof and risk documentation dominate—pair with `economic-value-and-pricing-logic.md` and `hbr-business-markets-value-propositions.md`.

## Core constructs (practical)

- **Job**: The progress the customer seeks, stated without forcing your product as the solution
- **Job steps**: Decompose into steps where struggle appears (slow, error-prone, costly)
- **Forces of progress**: Habits of the present, anxiety of the new, attraction, push events (useful in switch narratives; popularized in JTBD practice communities)

## Job story pattern (common template)

Use to keep propositions **situation-specific**:

> When **[circumstance]**, I want **[progress]**, so I can **[outcome]**.

## Agent practices

- Write **multiple job stories** when segments differ (enterprise vs mid-market, greenfield vs replacement).
- Separate **job** from **current workaround** (workaround reveals pains and reference alternatives).
- Map product behaviors to **job steps** (“orchestrates handoffs” vs “has feature X”).

## Example

- **Job**: Keep throughput stable while expanding automation vendors  
- **Struggle**: Each vendor optimizes locally; conflicts spike at handoffs  
- **Proposition direction**: Centralized orchestration that resolves cross-subsystem conflicts (tie to measurable coordination outcomes)
